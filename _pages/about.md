<details id="before-you-apply" class="internship-note">
  <summary>Before you apply</summary>
  <p><strong>Capacity, fit, and approval.</strong> Remote internships and in-person visits have very limited capacity. I spend substantial time with my students and research partners, so I consider only proposals with a strong research fit and clear mutual benefit. A remote internship is typically required before I consider an in-person visit. International and inter-institutional collaborations can involve additional risks; please consider carefully whether the proposed collaboration is necessary before applying. Research postgraduate students must notify their supervisor before contacting me and obtain the supervisor's written approval before I can commit to a position. Every position, including a remote internship, also requires written approval from my team leader and the Director of Research before I can commit.</p>
  <p><strong>Compensation and computing resources.</strong> Both remote internships and in-person visits are unpaid and include no salary, stipend, or reimbursement of any kind. In-person visitors may be granted access to NRC's top-tier, world-class computing resources, subject to further approval.</p>
  <p><strong>Research security and oversight.</strong> As a federal research institution, the NRC applies strict internal requirements to research security, and NRC-related activities and collaborations are subject to oversight and monitoring. Remote internships require a minimal, risk-based research-security review under the <a href="https://nrc.canada.ca/en/corporate/values-ethics/statement-research-security" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">NRC Statement on Research Security</a>; access to non-public NRC research information depends on that review. As a condition of an in-person visit, visitors must complete comprehensive screening for Government of Canada <a href="https://www.canada.ca/en/government/publicservice/workforce/staffing/security-screening.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Reliability Status</a> before the visit. International or inter-institutional projects may require additional review based on the project and planned activities.</p>
  <p><strong>Remote resources and intellectual property.</strong> Remote interns typically cannot access NRC computational resources. The NRC's <a href="https://www.nrc.canada.ca/en/research-development/intellectual-property-licensing/nrcs-intellectual-property-ip-policy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">intellectual-property policy</a> applies to work connected with NRC. Please disclose your institution's IP requirements before a project is defined. Where NRC policy permits, NRC may agree not to claim IP in a clearly defined part of work by a remote intern or visiting researcher, subject to written approval before work begins.</p>
  <p><strong>Visa sponsorship.</strong> We do not sponsor visas or visa applications of any kind. Applicants and visitors are responsible for obtaining the entry documents required for their circumstances.</p>
  <p><strong>In-person visits and immigration.</strong> An in-person visit is not NRC employment and does not authorize work. Immigration requirements depend on the planned activities; some unpaid activities may still count as work under <a href="https://www.ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=1288&amp;top=17" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">IRCC guidance</a>. I have consulted immigration counsel to define the boundaries for these visits. You must obtain any immigration authorization required for your circumstances before travel. For an in-person visit, I require any Canadian visitor visa you need to remain valid for at least six months after your planned arrival; see <a href="https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/visit-canada.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">IRCC's current entry-document guidance</a>. Students studying outside Canada without Canadian residency status must contact me at least six months before their planned visit. A presentation/talk is typically required, e.g. at the NRC's NLP seminar, before an in-person visit position can be committed.</p>
  <p><strong>Costs.</strong> Visitors are responsible for all visit-related expenses, including conference registration fees and fees charged for paid journal publication.</p>
  <p><strong>Documentation.</strong> Upon request, I can issue an official certificate or letter confirming your experience. This may serve as proof of experience or help meet your institution's overseas experience requirements.</p>
  <p><strong>Publication.</strong> An internship or visit does not guarantee a publication.</p>
